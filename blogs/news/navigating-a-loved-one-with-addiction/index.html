Supporting someone through addiction

Care and boundaries can exist at the same time. Here is a grounded way to support someone you love without disappearing from your own life.

General educationDoppler journal
In this articleStart with what is yours to control.Find treatment and supportWhen safety is urgentFind your support

When someone you love is struggling with substance use, it can be hard to know what helps. You may feel pulled between protecting them, confronting them, rescuing them, and stepping back.

Start with what is yours to control.

You can offer care, speak honestly, and help someone find resources. You cannot make every choice for another adult. Remembering that difference is not giving up; it can make your support more sustainable.

Be direct without becoming cruel.

Choose a calm moment. Describe what you have noticed in specific terms, explain how it affects you, and make room for the other person to respond.

Boundaries need an action.

A boundary is about what you will do, not only what you want someone else to stop doing. Keep it realistic. A therapist or support group can help you decide what protects your safety, finances, home, and emotional health.

Remember

You deserve support too.

Loving someone through addiction can be isolating. Your own therapist can give you a private place to process fear, anger, guilt, grief, and the decisions in front of you.

Find treatment and support

FindTreatment.gov is a confidential, anonymous U.S. treatment locator from the Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration.

When safety is urgent

In the U.S., call 911 for an overdose or immediate danger. For immediate emotional support, call or text 988. Elsewhere, use local emergency and crisis services.

About this article

This article offers general education, not a diagnosis, treatment plan, or replacement for professional care. Talk with a qualified professional about your circumstances.
