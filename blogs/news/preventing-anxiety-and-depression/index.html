Support your mental health before it feels too heavy

Mental health challenges are not always preventable. But regular support, connection, rest, and early attention can make it easier to respond.

Anxiety and depression are not personal failures, and no routine guarantees they will never happen. Still, caring for your mental health before a breaking point can help you notice changes and ask for support sooner.

Know your early signals.

Changes in sleep, appetite, energy, focus, patience, motivation, or connection may be worth noticing. One difficult week does not define you, but a pattern can be useful information.

Protect the basics without making them another burden.

Regular sleep, movement, nourishing food, daylight, and time with supportive people can matter. Start with the smallest version that feels realistic.

Stay connected.

Isolation can make hard feelings feel even larger. A trusted person, community, therapist, or support group can help you carry what is happening.

Early care counts

You do not have to prove it is “bad enough.”

Wanting support is enough of a reason to talk with a mental health professional.

Get urgent help when you need it.

In the U.S., call 911 if you are in immediate danger. For free, confidential emotional support, call or text 988 at any time. Elsewhere, use local emergency and crisis services.
