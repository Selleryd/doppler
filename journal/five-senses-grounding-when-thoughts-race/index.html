Grounding / short guide

Five-senses grounding when thoughts race

A flexible way to notice what is around you—without having to empty your mind.

A sequence, not a rule.

This exercise gives you a few small things to notice in a safe, familiar space. It is not a treatment or a guarantee of relief. You can stop, skip steps, repeat a step, or choose a different sense. Keep your breathing natural.

Notice at your own pace.

Look for five things you can see. Notice four comfortable points of contact, such as your feet on the floor or fabric on your skin. Listen for three sounds. Choose two familiar scents—or two colors instead. Finally, notice a taste already present, or rest your attention on one neutral object. You do not need to eat, drink, or search for a sensation that is uncomfortable.

Adapt the exercise to you.

There is no prize for finishing the sequence. Sensory differences, disability, and your preferences all belong in the exercise. The guided grounding tool lets you move through the steps manually. If focusing on sensations increases distress, stop and choose another kind of support.
