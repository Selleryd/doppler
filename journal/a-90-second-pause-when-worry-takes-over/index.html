Anxiety / short guide

A 90-second pause when worry takes over

A small place to put your attention when your thoughts seem to be moving faster than the room around you.

Begin with the room, not the whole problem.

You do not have to solve the worry before you are allowed to take a moment. Try sitting or standing somewhere safe. Keep your eyes open if that feels more comfortable. Choose one ordinary detail in front of you: the edge of a table, a familiar color, or the light on the floor.

Make the next minute smaller.

For roughly thirty seconds, let your attention return to that one detail. Then notice where your body is supported by a chair, the floor, or another surface. There is no special breathing pattern to get right. For the last part of the pause, ask yourself: “What is the next small thing in front of me?” That might be getting a glass of water, writing down one question, or telling someone that you need support.

A pause is not a promise.

Nothing has to feel transformed at the end. A brief exercise is a choice, not a test you pass by feeling calm. Stop if the exercise feels uncomfortable. If worry is interfering with daily life, bring that experience to a qualified health professional rather than relying on a website exercise alone.

Background resource: NCCIH — Meditation and mindfulness: effectiveness and safety. The exercise or reflection above is an editorial prompt, not a validated clinical protocol.
