Grounding / short guide

When focusing on your breath feels worse

You do not have to use your breath as an anchor. Start with something outside yourself instead.

You are allowed to stop.

An exercise that feels uncomfortable does not become more useful because you force yourself to finish it. Let your breathing return to its own rhythm. Open your eyes, change position, or leave the exercise entirely. Your response is information, not a personal failure.

Choose a neutral detail.

Look for something ordinary in the space around you: a window frame, the color of a mug, a quiet sound, or the feeling of the chair beneath you. You can name the detail silently and move on. Skip any sensation that feels difficult or unavailable to you.

Bring the experience into the conversation.

You could tell a clinician, “Focusing on breathing makes me feel more uncomfortable. Can we try a different approach?” Mindfulness practices are not one-size-fits-all. NCCIH notes that some people have negative experiences with meditation and mindfulness and advises against using them to replace or delay conventional care.

Background resource: NCCIH — Meditation and mindfulness: effectiveness and safety. The exercise or reflection above is an editorial prompt, not a validated clinical protocol.
