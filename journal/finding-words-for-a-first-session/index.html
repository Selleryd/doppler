Therapy / short guide

Finding words for a first session

You do not need a complete history or a polished explanation to begin a conversation.

Start with a sentence that is true.

“I am not sure where to begin.” “I have been more tired than usual.” “The same argument keeps happening.” “Something has felt different, and I want help understanding it.” These are possible openings—not a script you have to use. Choose the words that sound like you.

Write down what you want to remember.

Before an appointment, you might note what has become harder, what you hope support could change, and what would help you feel comfortable in the room. A short note can also include questions about the clinician’s approach, privacy, session format, fees, and what happens if the match does not feel right.

Leave room for questions.

A first conversation can be a chance to discuss goals and ask how the therapist works. You are allowed to say that you need more time, that a question is unclear, or that you are not ready to talk about a particular experience. For background on psychotherapy and questions to ask a prospective provider, see the NIMH resource below.
