Making room for grief, without a script

A few words to bring into a conversation when other people’s expectations do not fit your experience.

Say what the loss has changed.

There may be a person, relationship, role, routine, or imagined future you miss. You can talk about the practical changes as well as the feelings. “Mornings feel different now.” “I do not know how to answer when people ask how I am.” “I miss having someone to call.” Start wherever the experience feels most real.

Ask for the kind of company you need.

You might want someone to listen, help with an errand, sit nearby, or stop offering advice for a while. Consider a simple request: “Could you listen without trying to make this better?” or “Can you check in with me next week?” Use your own language; these are examples, not obligations.

Bring the unfinished parts.

A conversation does not need a lesson, a timeline, or a tidy ending. You can bring mixed feelings and unanswered questions to therapy. The starting point is not how someone else believes you should be doing. It is what life feels like for you, and what support would be useful now.

General reflection and practical questions—not a diagnosis, clinical screening, or treatment protocol. Discuss individual care needs with a qualified professional.
