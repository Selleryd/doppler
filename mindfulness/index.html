More choice in the moment

Calm is not the price of admission.

The aim is not to empty your mind or stay calm all the time. It is to become a little more aware of what is here before the next automatic reaction.

When it fits your care, you and your clinician can discuss whether grounding, breathing, reflection, or meditation might be useful. You can always ask for another approach.

01

Notice sooner.

You can begin by noticing a thought, feeling, or body signal without having to change it immediately.

02

Create a pause.

Choose a small, manageable practice—or simply stop for a moment and look around.

03

Choose what follows.

Bring what you notice back into a conversation about your needs, goals, and next steps.

A practice should make room for you.

These website exercises are general-wellness tools, not a substitute for assessment or treatment. Meditation and mindfulness can feel uncomfortable for some people. Do not use them to postpone needed care.

Stop any exercise that increases distress. Ask a qualified professional about an approach that fits your circumstances.
